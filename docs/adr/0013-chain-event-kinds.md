# Chain-event kinds: evidence, window and ownership

Status: proposed

A chain-event kind attests that an event was observed on a chain, such as a deposit at a Cardano script address. This ADR fixes the contract every chain-event kind must follow, the open points of #158. The indexer choice is its own decision; this ADR names none. Critical decisions are marked; a law or app that weakens one breaks the family's guarantees. Decisions that depart from other ADRs are marked as divergences.

**Critical. Attesting an event grants no ownership.** The claim certifies that the event happened, not who it belongs to: the pointer is public, so any observer can request attestations for the same event. The unsalted nullifier means a pending submission reveals the event it attests, so a griefer can target it. The derived window and values make the second submission of one event a replay, so the race is about who lands first. Which holder may claim an event is a predicate decision, not a kind guarantee: the acceptance and the app's predicate run in one atomic contract call, so a submission whose holder the predicate rejects reverts and burns no nullifier. The race only bites an app whose predicate accepts any holder; there first-come-first-served is the semantics the app chose. An app that grants per event binds the claimant in its own predicate (ADR 0001). A law that must hand ownership of an event extends its evidence with a proof of control of an address in the event, or a source-chain signature over the claim context checked by the attestor.

**Critical. A claim certifies observation, not finality.** Each attestor signs the block it saw, so while a fork is open, attestors on either side sign different subjects and no attestment forms; the losing branch rolls back when the fork resolves. A reorganization costs liveness, not the claim's honesty: it remains a true record of what was observed, and depth makes a rolled-back block rare. But an accepted claim outlives its block, and the chain no longer holds what the claim attests. The app owns finality: depth in the policy, a bound on the block number and slot in its predicate, and the residual risk stays.

**Critical. Diverges from ADR 0012. The nullifier carries no salt.** ADR 0012 salts zkFetch's nullifier because its values are secret and guessable. A chain event's values are public chain data: the block time, the amount, and the address all sit on the source chain. There is nothing to guess. Every field the nullifier hashes is public, and an observer who enumerates events can match the nullifier to its event. A secret in the nullifier would hide only that linkage. The secrets that could hold it are a key shared by the set's operators, which must be distributed and rotated, or a change to the shared module that weakens replay for other kinds; this ADR declines both, answering ADR 0012's closing paragraph for this family. The cost is event linkage: the nullifier on the ledger, and a pending submission, names the event a claim attests. An observer who suspects an event confirms the link with one hash. An observer who starts from the nullifier must scan for its event: the app's `freshnessWindow` bounds the event to a recent span of blocks, and the address the app watches is public chain data, so candidates are few. An app that cannot bear the linkage motivates a salted variant.

**Diverges from ADR 0005. The window is the event's block time.** The law derives `windowStart = windowEnd` from the block's time; the proposal carries no window. Every attestor of one event derives the same window, so they sign the same message. ADR 0005 reads differently for this family because the observation and the fact share the block, so the fact's time is the observation's time. The contract's checks still hold. The claim is submittable once the block exists and for the app's sealed `freshnessWindow` after it.

**One event mints one claim per app and policy.** Same event, same subject, same window, same values, same nullifier, whatever the holder. The window and values are derived from the event, and the observation identity excludes the holder (ADR 0006), so within one app and one policy the contract's replay check rejects the second acceptance. A reorg moves the block and with it the subject, so a re-claimed event is a new fact; an app that wants once-per-event across reorgs keys its state on the event's own identity fields, such as `txHash` and `outputIndex`, not on `subjectHash`. The dupe hole of #112 does not exist for this family.

**One law per chain, one policy variant per event.** A law covers one chain, because the chain is what a law owns: the evidence payload, a transaction however located, its parser, its confirmation depth unit, and its value encodings. A union law across chains would branch its verify per chain, and any one chain's schema change would bump the law tag and invalidate every chain's policy hashes. Whether the event is a transfer, a mint, or a delegation is policy. The policy is a closed union over the chain's event shapes, one variant per shape, each carrying its identity fields, check parameters and slot map, plus the depth and the pins the app sets such as the address to watch, and the variant tag rides in the policy hash. Adding a variant is additive and needs no law bump. Below the law every kind shares the keyless claim and its hashes (`@thoth/core`) and `defineAttestor` (`@thoth/attestor`).

**The evidence is the locator, not the payload.** The law fixes which fields the client sends: the minimal input it needs to find the event. The attestor re-reads the event from its own endpoint and derives every value from what it read, so what the client sends can only locate the event, never shape the claim (ADR 0007).

**The block is part of the subject.** The subject includes the block the event landed in, alongside the network and the event's own identity. The block number and the slot are fact positions, so they are values, not part of the subject (ADR 0005).

**Diverges from ADR 0011. No witness binds the claim context.** ADR 0011 binds the holder and window through a witness-signed digest because zkFetch's evidence is reusable and signed by a third party. Chain-event evidence is the attestor's own read at attest time, and with window derived from the event, nothing client-chosen can steer a second claim. The client's proposal contributes only `appId`, the holder and the counts. The family achieves what ADR 0011 achieves by derivation instead of binding.

**The trust model is independent sources, no proofs for now.** Each attestor reads from its own endpoint; attestors that share one count as one (ADR 0003). This ADR defers header proofs and light clients.

**The first law is `cardano@1`.** Its first shape attests one UTxO at a script address. It attests the event, not ownership. Later laws instantiate the same contract on other chains; new event shapes join `cardano@1` as new policy variants.
