# The nullifier keys on the observation identity, not a client nonce

Status: accepted, amended by [ADR 0012](0012-nullifier-hashes-a-secret-attestor-derived-salt.md): the observation identity also includes a secret salt the attestors derive from the evidence. A kind may derive the salt from the holder, as zkFetch does. Then a fresh holder gets a fresh nullifier, and the nullifier no longer stops one fact from being claimed again under a fresh holder, even in the same window. The evidence binding of [ADR 0011](0011-evidence-binds-the-claim-context.md) stops only the reuse of evidence signed for another holder. Also amended by [ADR 0013](0013-contract-seals-its-app-id.md): each contract keeps its own nullifier set, so the app id in the identity does not stop replay into a different app; the contract's sealed app id does.

The claim carries no nonce. The contract derives the nullifier from the observation identity the attestors signed: the app id, `policyHash`, `subjectHash`, window, the numeric and byte values, and `present`. It excludes `holder`, so a fresh holder secret cannot be used to re-claim the same fact (this no longer holds where the salt follows the holder; see the status line). No client-chosen randomness, because a client could vary it to mint several claims for one fact. Uniqueness follows from the agreed fields: a fetch is unique by its response and observation time, an event by its identity. The app id is part of the identity, which separates the claims of different apps within one nullifier set; each contract keeps its own set, so this does not stop a claim from being replayed into a different app (see the status line).
